Regions and grape varieties

Not an encyclopedia, but the parts that matter once the bottle is already bought: how long will it keep, and how do I recognise the right moment?

Barolo and Barbaresco: two zones, one grape, a great deal of patience

Few wines punish impatience as plainly as these two — and few repay it as reliably.

Blaufränkisch: dark fruit, firm acidity and three Burgenland signatures

Few red varieties wear their origin this openly, and few stretch this far from litre bottle to single-vineyard wine.

Bordeaux: left bank, right bank, and what it means for your cellar

No other name on the shelf covers so much ground: everyday bottles finished at three years, and wines that have barely started at twenty.

Burgenland: four sub-regions, two kinds of wine, very different ageing curves

Treat Burgenland as one region and you will shelve bottles side by side whose drinking windows are a decade apart.

Chianti Classico: the pyramid, the black rooster and the ageing question

A decade separates the everyday bottle from the one worth shelf space, even though both carry the same name.

Grüner Veltliner: the pepper note, the range, and a badly underrated cellar wine

Austria's most planted variety puts two completely different wines into the same bottle shape, and only one of them rewards years in the cellar.

Kamptal: loess, the Heiligenstein and an underrated capacity to age

Half an hour from the Wachau grows a different kind of wine — cooler, spicier, and routinely drunk years before it has finished.

Nebbiolo: pale in the glass, formidable in the mouth

No other red variety misleads so completely — judge one by its colour and the first mouthful will catch you unprepared.

Pinot Noir: the thin-skinned grape and its awkward ageing curve

Little colour, little tannin, plenty of acidity — and still one of the longest-lived red grapes. That contradiction explains nearly everything that matters in a cellar.

Riesling: acidity, residual sugar and the question of petrol

Almost no white variety keeps this long or this reliably — and almost none is judged so often by the wrong marker on the label.

Sangiovese: one grape, many wines, very different windows

The same grape sits behind a simple Rosso and a Brunello — separated by a decade of patience.

South Styria: steep slopes, high acidity, and whites that keep longer than expected

The region has a reputation for young, fresh whites, which is accurate for half of what it makes and thoroughly misleading for the rest.

Wachau: Steinfeder, Federspiel, Smaragd — and which of them keeps

In most regions the vineyard name decides how long a wine lasts; in the Wachau it is a single word describing ripeness at harvest.

Zweigelt: Austria's most-drunk red, and why it rarely wants waiting

The commonest mistake with this variety is not poor storage but storage that lasts too long.
